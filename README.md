# Data-Privacy-Audit on Instagram 
Data Privacy Audit of Instagram
1. Introduction

Instagram is a social-media platform owned by Meta Platforms. It processes a large amount of personal and behavioral information in order to provide its services, maintain security, personalize content, and deliver advertising.

This privacy audit focuses on the practical privacy risks associated with cookies, permissions, personal-data collection, tracking, third-party sharing, account security, and user controls.

Overall assessment: Instagram provides reasonable security and several privacy controls, but the platform presents a high privacy exposure because of the breadth of information that can be collected and used for personalization and advertising.

2. Data Collected by Instagram

Instagram may process information such as:

Name, username, email address and profile information
Photos, videos, comments, messages and other content
Likes, follows, searches and other interactions
Information about accounts and people with whom a user interacts
Device information
IP address and network information
Information about how the user interacts with Meta products
Information received from partners and other sources


Privacy risk: HIGH

The main concern is not only the information directly entered by the user. Instagram can generate a detailed behavioral profile from interactions, interests and usage patterns.

3. Cookies and Similar Technologies

Cookies are small files or identifiers stored by websites in a user's browser. Instagram/Meta uses cookies and similar technologies for functions such as:

Authentication and maintaining sessions
Security and fraud prevention
Remembering preferences
Measuring and understanding usage
Personalization
Advertising-related purposes

Therefore, cookies are not inherently dangerous. Some are essential for the website to function securely.

However, tracking technologies can become a privacy concern when they are used to understand a user's activity across websites, services or devices.

Privacy risk: MEDIUM–HIGH

Practical finding: Users should distinguish between essential cookies required for functionality and cookies/technologies used for analytics, personalization or advertising.

4. Website Tracking and Third-Party Data

One of the most important privacy concerns is that Meta's data ecosystem is not limited to activity occurring directly on Instagram.

Meta describes receiving information from partners about people's activity on other websites, apps and services. This can occur through technologies such as Meta Pixel, APIs and SDKs.

This can allow information from businesses and other services to contribute to Meta's advertising and personalization systems.

Privacy risk: HIGH

This does not mean Instagram can automatically see everything a person does on the internet.

Instead, the concern is that participating websites and applications may send information to Meta, creating additional data points associated with users or devices.

5. App Permissions

The Instagram mobile application can request access to device capabilities such as:

Camera
Microphone
Photos/media
Location
Notifications
Contacts

A permission does not necessarily mean that Instagram is continuously using that resource.

For example, camera or microphone access should be controlled through the operating system, and users can review or revoke permissions.

Recommended practical configuration
Permission	Recommendation	Risk
Camera	Allow only when needed	Medium
Microphone	Allow only when needed	Medium
Photos	Prefer selected photos where supported	Medium
Location	Deny unless required	Medium–High
Contacts	Disable if unnecessary	High
Notifications	Personal preference	Low
Background activity	Restrict where practical	Medium

Important: Contact access deserves particular attention because an address book contains information about other people as well as the user.


6. Account Security

Privacy also depends on account security.

If an Instagram account is compromised, an attacker could potentially gain access to private content, messages, personal information and account activity.

Recommended protections include:

Strong, unique password
Two-factor authentication
Review of login activity
Removal of unknown devices/sessions
Up-to-date recovery information
Caution with phishing messages and fake verification requests
Privacy risk: MEDIUM

Security controls can significantly reduce the risk of unauthorized account access.
